## Scope

How a backtest decides what was known when and what it trades: the ordering gate in front of the LLM backfill, point-in-time alignment, entry and holding-window timing, one event per corroboration, trading only universe members, windows the prices do not cover, the delisting bound and the survivorship flag, and the cross-sectional portfolio engine that trades score snapshots.

The event path is parked while its promotable hypotheses are `suspended`; read each hypothesis's `status` from its `config/hypotheses/h*.yaml` file. The code runs, so its rules are stated here as current behaviour.

Not in this file: trial accounting, inference, kill criteria, verdicts, the holdout and variant reporting ([evaluation-protocol.md](evaluation-protocol.md)); fills and costs ([simulation.md](simulation.md)); the universe lists ([universe.md](universe.md)); the score panels ([panels.md](panels.md)); the corroboration window and how corroboration records are stored ([corroboration.md](../core-hub/corroboration.md)); the per-source publication-date mapping ([connectors.md](../ingestion/connectors.md)).

## Requirements

- <a id="RES-066"></a>**RES-066** — The LLM historical backfill does not start until the H11 and H12 diagnostics have been read and show that catalyst windows or registry edits carry return; the two non-LLM panels they read are built first.
- <a id="RES-067"></a>**RES-067** — Processing time never decides what a backtest knows: alignment uses only a signal's `published_date`, or a corroboration's `corroborated_at` (the latest of its participants' `published_date`), and a join on a processing-time field (`ingested_at`, `retrieved_at`, `first_detected_at`) raises.
- <a id="RES-068"></a>**RES-068** — Dates that were not public never align a signal: a patent aligns on its publication date, not its filing date, and a trial on its post date, not its submission date, so no signal enters a price window that predates its disclosure.
- <a id="RES-069"></a>**RES-069** — An event enters at the first NYSE session whose open is after its timestamp; a date-only timestamp is known after that day's close. A holding window of N days runs in calendar days from the entry open to the close of `next_trading_day(entry + N)`. A missing price row gives `None`, not an error, in a backtest without a universe.
- <a id="RES-070"></a>**RES-070** — A timestamp of exactly `00:00:00` UTC is treated as date-only, known after that day's close.
- <a id="RES-071"></a>**RES-071** — A backtest has one event per corroboration, not one per superseding record: a signal joining an existing corroboration adds no event, a disjoint later corroboration on the same target is a new event, events are per ticker, and each event's return runs from its own entry after its latest participant. The full variant's weight uses only the participants known at the event, and a superseded corroboration keeps its own participant set, so the set can be read as of a date. One document extracted several times counts once.
- <a id="RES-072"></a>**RES-072** — With a universe, only events on companies that were members in their entry month are traded; the others are dropped and counted. A window past a delisted member's last price ends at that close with its exit reason when the member's coverage is complete; a window the prices do not cover on a partial or unpriced member, an event after a member's last trade and a gap inside a window are excluded and counted by exit reason (no neighbouring close is borrowed); a fully priced, still-listed member that runs out of data raises; a window ending after the universe's `as_of` is pending, neither kept nor excluded. Exits and coverage are read from the latest listings, and membership without stored listings is refused. Both corroboration variants read the same universe.
- <a id="RES-073"></a>**RES-073** — Each metrics report carries a survivorship summary: excluded events by exit reason, a bounded mean that puts excluded events back at −30% (delisted, deregistered) or 0% (acquired) over their unpriced part and flat for a still-listed data gap, and `survivorship_gap = material` when more than 10% of events are excluded. Both constants live in code (`services/backtesting/src/auspex_backtesting/metrics/survivorship.py`), not in a registered file.
- <a id="RES-074"></a>**RES-074** — An event backtest is deterministic for identical inputs and runs without network access; a run with no corroborated signal gives an empty report, not an error.

*Portfolio engine.*

- <a id="RES-075"></a>**RES-075** — A score snapshot is one Parquet per score and rebalance date at `scores/{hypothesis_id}/{version}/{yyyy-mm-dd}.parquet` in `auspex-prices`, with `cik`, `ticker`, `score` (higher ranks higher) and `known_at_max` (UTC). `ScoreSnapshot.validate()` refuses a row known at or after the rebalance session's open, on write and on every read by the engine, and `ScoreStore.write()` refuses to overwrite a snapshot.
- <a id="RES-076"></a>**RES-076** — A decision date is the first NYSE session of the month, or the quarterly or semi-annual trade date after the 13F deadline that the hypothesis registers; it reads that month's universe snapshot and the latest score snapshot dated on or before it, and a newer score waits for the next decision date. Selection is `top_n` with hold bands (buy at the entry rank, keep while at the exit rank; equal ranks are a hard cut), `top_quintile` or `exclude_bottom_quintile`, ties broken by CIK, with an optional exclusion score whose bottom quintile is neither bought nor kept. Orders fill at the next session's open, sells first, and buys are scaled to the cash left, so the account never borrows.
- <a id="RES-077"></a>**RES-077** — `EqualRiskSizer` weights by inverse trailing volatility from sessions strictly before the decision date, capped at the protocol's `sizing.max_position_pct`; what the caps leave is cash earning nothing, never renormalised above the cap. Fewer names than `sizing.min_names` raises.
- <a id="RES-078"></a>**RES-078** — `CatalystGuard` has four modes: `none`, `skip_entry`, `exit_and_reenter` and `exit_low_score_only`. A name with no catalyst data known at the decision is counted, never treated as clear, and imprecise catalyst periods count by overlap.
- <a id="RES-079"></a>**RES-079** — Each order is costed at the capital tier's size, every tier separately, and a new position below the minimum ticket is skipped and counted.
- <a id="RES-080"></a>**RES-080** — A held name whose prices stop is closed at its last close; a `delisted` or `deregistered` exit whose prices stop early is booked at `DELISTING_TAIL_RETURN` (`services/backtesting/src/auspex_backtesting/portfolio/engine.py`) and its month flagged.
- <a id="RES-081"></a>**RES-081** — `PortfolioResult` reports per month gross and net return, cost, turnover, names held, cash share, the benchmark and universe equal-weight returns and the excess over each, plus cost by component, yearly cost drag, average holding period, returns near known day-precision binaries versus other days, delisting-bound months, skipped tickets, unpriced member-months and the score quintile spread. Identical inputs give an identical result.
- <a id="RES-082"></a>**RES-082** — The score version a portfolio run reads matches its trial cell's `floor` axis: a floored cell reads floored snapshots and a `no_floor` cell reads the no-floor version.

Variant reporting for every result built on corroborations is an evaluation rule: [EVA-022](evaluation-protocol.md#EVA-022).

## Rationale

- **Why a historical backfill at all.** Requirements §0.3: forward-only collection yields too few corroborations for a backtest within any useful time, so the evidence has to come from a backfill over past documents, with every look-ahead guard above applied to it.
- **Why the event backtest joins on `corroborated_at`.** Requirements §4.1 defines it as the latest participant's publication time, the first instant the corroboration could have been seen; any later processing time would put the entry after news the market already priced, and any earlier one would trade on a corroboration that did not exist yet.
